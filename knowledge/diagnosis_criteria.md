# Diagnosis Criteria and Workup for Alzheimer's Disease

## NIA-AA Diagnostic Framework (2011 / 2018)

The National Institute on Aging and Alzheimer's Association (NIA-AA) framework
recognizes Alzheimer's disease as a biological continuum:

- **Preclinical AD:** biomarker evidence of amyloid/tau pathology without
  clinical symptoms.
- **MCI due to AD:** objective cognitive impairment with preserved daily
  function plus biomarker evidence.
- **AD dementia:** objective cognitive impairment that interferes with daily
  activities plus biomarker evidence.

A diagnosis is probabilistic and multidisciplinary: it combines history from the
patient and an informant, cognitive testing, functional assessment, physical and
neurological examination, laboratory workup, and neuroimaging.

## Typical Diagnostic Workup

1. History and collateral information (usually from a family member).
2. Cognitive screening: MMSE, MoCA.
3. Functional assessment: ability to perform activities of daily living.
4. Clinical staging: CDR scale.
5. Structural neuroimaging: MRI to assess hippocampal/whole-brain atrophy and
   exclude other causes (tumors, vascular disease, normal pressure
   hydrocephalus).
6. Laboratory tests to rule out reversible causes (thyroid, B12, infection).
7. Genetic testing for APOE-epsilon4 when relevant.

## Red Flags Requiring Specialist Referral

- Rapidly progressive symptoms (weeks to months).
- Focal neurological signs.
- New seizures or gait disturbance.
- Onset before 65 with strong family history.
- Marked behavioral or psychiatric symptoms.

## Caveats for AI-Assisted Prediction

An automated prediction from imaging and clinical scores is a decision-support
tool, not a definitive diagnosis. It should always be interpreted in the full
clinical context and confirmed by a qualified clinician. Imaging-only
classifications can be affected by image quality, slice position, and the
patient's age and head size.
