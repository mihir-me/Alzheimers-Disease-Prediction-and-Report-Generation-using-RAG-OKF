# Alzheimer's Disease: Overview and Stages

## What is Alzheimer's Disease

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder and the
most common cause of dementia, accounting for roughly 60-80% of dementia cases.
It is characterized by the accumulation of extracellular amyloid-beta plaques
and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles of hyperphosphorylated tau protein,
leading to synaptic loss, neuronal death, and progressive cognitive decline.

## Disease Progression

Alzheimer's follows a gradual continuum from preclinical disease through mild
cognitive impairment (MCI) to dementia. The clinical course is typically
measured in years, and the rate of progression varies between individuals.

## Clinical Severity Stages

Clinicians commonly describe four broad stages based on the Clinical Dementia
Rating (CDR) scale and cognitive testing:

- **No Impairment (CDR 0):** No cognitive decline; the individual functions
  normally in daily life. Memory and problem-solving are within normal limits.
  This corresponds to a normal or "Non Demented" label in imaging datasets.

- **Very Mild Impairment (CDR 0.5):** Questionable or very mild decline.
  The patient may have slight forgetfulness, difficulty recalling recent events,
  or mild difficulty with complex tasks. Daily function is largely preserved.
  This is sometimes called mild cognitive impairment (MCI) when it does not yet
  meet dementia criteria.

- **Mild Impairment (CDR 1):** Mild dementia. Clear memory loss, difficulty with
  problem solving and complex tasks, and noticeable impact on work or social
  activities. The patient can usually still live independently with support.

- **Moderate Impairment (CDR 2):** Moderate dementia. Significant memory loss,
  confusion, difficulty with language and recognition, and greater dependence
  on caregivers for daily activities.

## Imaging in Alzheimer's

Structural MRI is the most widely used imaging modality. Typical findings
include bilateral atrophy of the medial temporal lobe, particularly the
hippocampus and entorhinal cortex, enlargement of the lateral ventricles, and
generalized cortical atrophy that progresses with disease severity.
