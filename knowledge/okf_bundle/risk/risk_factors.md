# Major Risk Factors

- **Age:** the single strongest risk factor; incidence rises steeply after 65.
- **Genetics:** APOE-epsilon4 allele increases risk and lowers the age of onset
  (one copy moderate, two copies higher risk). Rare autosomal dominant
  mutations (APP, PSEN1, PSEN2) cause early-onset familial AD.
- **Family history:** a first-degree relative with AD increases lifetime risk.
- **Cardiovascular factors:** hypertension, diabetes, obesity, dyslipidemia,
  and smoking are associated with higher risk and likely worsen the pathology.
- **Education / cognitive reserve:** lower educational attainment and limited
  lifelong cognitive engagement are associated with a higher risk of clinical
  dementia, though the neuropathology may be similar.
- **Head injury, hearing loss, depression, and social isolation** are
  additional modifiable or contextual risk factors.
