# Treatment and Management of Alzheimer's Disease

## Cholinesterase Inhibitors

Donepezil, rivastigmine, and galantamine are approved for mild-to-moderate AD.
They raise acetylcholine levels and provide modest symptomatic benefit for
cognition and global function. They do not modify the underlying disease.

## NMDA Receptor Antagonist

Memantine is approved for moderate-to-severe AD and is often used in
combination with a cholinesterase inhibitor. It modulates glutamatergic
signaling and may delay functional decline.

## Disease-Modifying Therapies (anti-amyloid)

Monoclonal antibodies targeting amyloid-beta (e.g., lecanemab) are newer
treatments for early AD that slow cognitive decline modestly. They require
careful selection (amyloid confirmation, MRI monitoring for amyloid-related
imaging abnormalities) and are not universally available.

## Non-Pharmacological Management

- Cognitive stimulation and structured daily routines.
- Physical exercise and cardiovascular risk-factor control.
- Social engagement.
- Caregiver education and support.
- Management of behavioral and psychological symptoms with non-drug strategies
  first.

## Lifestyle and Risk Modification

- Blood pressure, diabetes, and cholesterol control.
- Mediterranean-style diet.
- Smoking cessation and moderation of alcohol.
- Adequate sleep and management of depression.

## When the Model Predicts Early-Stage Disease

If the model flags Very Mild Impairment (MCI-like), appropriate next steps
include formal cognitive assessment, vascular risk optimization, monitoring of
progression, and discussion of early intervention options with a specialist.
